10 Questions to Ask a Fort Dodge Water Damage Company

Ten plain questions help you sort a careful restoration company from one that cuts corners, and none of them take long.

By the Fort Dodge Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026

When water is on the floor, you tend to hire the first person who answers the phone. That is understandable, and it is also when people get burned. A few good questions, asked calmly, tell you most of what you need to know about who you are letting into your home.

Here are ten questions, in the order that works best. You can ask them by phone before anyone drives out. A good company will answer them without hesitation.

Questions about the company

  1. Are you licensed and insured? Ask for proof of insurance, and ask what licensing Iowa requires for the work. Do not accept a vague answer.
  2. Who will actually be on site? Find out whether the crew works for the company or is subcontracted, and who supervises.
  3. How soon can you start? Get a plain answer. Be wary of any guarantee that sounds too exact, because traffic, weather and other jobs can change timing.

These three tell you whether you are dealing with a real operation. Answers should be short, specific and easy to repeat.

Questions about the work

  1. Which standard do you follow? Reputable firms work to the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard and can explain what that means.
  2. What category is the water, and how do you know? The answer should mention the source and the risk.
  3. What equipment will you use and how do you decide when it is dry? Look for moisture meters, air movers, dehumidifiers and written dry goals.
  4. What will you remove, and why? Ask about carpet pad, baseboards and drywall, and what can stay.

Compare answers between companies. If one says everything must come out and another says most of it can be dried in place, ask each to explain their reasoning.

Questions about the paperwork

  1. Will I get a written scope and estimate before work starts? It should list rooms, tasks, equipment and what is excluded.
  2. What records will I get at the end? Look for moisture logs, photos and equipment records, which support an insurance claim.
  3. What do you need from me, and what do you ask me to sign? Read any form carefully, and never sign away your claim rights.

A company that welcomes the paperwork question is usually one that does the paperwork. For a deeper look at the estimate, see structural drying and how readings are tracked.

Red flags worth walking away from

  • Pressure to sign today, or an offer that expires in an hour.
  • No written scope, or a scope that is just a number.
  • Claims that they can guarantee your insurer will approve the claim.
  • Demand for a large cash deposit before any work.
  • Door-to-door solicitation right after a storm.

You can always take ten minutes. Water damage is urgent, but not so urgent that a thirty-second signature is required.

Why the order of these questions works

The first three questions screen out the worst operators in under two minutes. The next four show how the company thinks about the job. The last three test how transparent they are about paperwork and about what they will ask of you.

If you only have time for three, ask about insurance, ask which standard they follow and ask what records you will receive. Those answers separate the careful from the careless very quickly.

Keep the call short. Say where the water is coming from, what you have done and whether anyone is in danger. Then ask your questions while the crew heads out.

How to compare answers from two companies

Write each answer down. Compare how specific they are. A good answer names the equipment, the standard, the documents and the steps. A weak answer says trust us or we do this every day.

Do not reward the lowest price on its own. Look at what each company includes. One may include monitoring and a written dry standard, while another leaves them out. The cheaper quote may be missing the very steps that keep the job from failing.

If both seem solid, go with the company that explained its process most clearly and answered without pressure.

What a good call sounds like

A good intake call is calm and practical. The person asks where the water came from, how long it has been there, what rooms are affected and whether it is safe to be in the house. They tell you to stop the water, turn off power if needed and move valuables. They explain what the crew will do on arrival.

That kind of call tells you the company has a process. If you are dealing with a burst pipe, our guide to burst pipe cleanup shows what that process looks like in practice.

Fort Dodge note: check local requirements

Before you hire for repairs after the drying, ask the City of Fort Dodge Development Services Department, which houses the Code Enforcement Division at 819 1st Ave S, whether your repair needs a permit and whether the contractor must be registered. Requirements can change, so ask directly.

Residents of nearby towns should ask their own city hall. That includes Eagle Grove and Gowrie.

Questions from Fort Dodge homeowners

Should I get more than one quote?

If time allows, yes. For active flooding, stop the water first and call at least one qualified company right away.

Is the cheapest quote the best one?

Not always. Compare scopes line by line, because a low number may leave out drying or removal steps.

Can a restoration company deal directly with my insurer?

Many will talk to your insurer on your behalf with your permission, but the claim stays between you and your insurer.

What if the company I called does not answer these ten questions?

Call another. A company worth hiring will answer them plainly.
