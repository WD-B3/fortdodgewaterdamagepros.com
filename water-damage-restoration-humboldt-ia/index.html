Humboldt, IA sits where two forks of the Des Moines River meet, and the city has dealt with river water since its earliest years. A flood in 1867 destroyed the town's dam, and the river ran high again in 2024. That history is the reason water damage restoration in Humboldt starts with the river and the weather, not only with pipes.

Our 24/7 emergency line reaches Humboldt from Fort Dodge, about 17 miles south, and same-day response is offered when available. Humboldt has 4,792 residents, and it is in Humboldt County, not Webster County, so permits and offices differ from Fort Dodge.

Spring melt, summer storms and the river

River towns feel the seasons in the water table. Snowmelt and spring rain can raise the river, and summer thunderstorms can overload drains and sump pumps. If water enters a home from outside, it is treated as contaminated, and the flood damage cleanup page explains what that means for carpet, drywall and insulation.

Frank A. Gotch Park sits at the meeting of the river's two forks, and homes close to that area are the ones most likely to think about floodplains. Humboldt sits at about 1,079 feet above sea level, and the ground is mostly flat to gently rolling, so drainage depends on grading and storm sewers. Check the FEMA flood map for your address.

When water is spreading in Humboldt

Do not wait for the river to crest or the rain to stop before you call. Safety comes first, and calling comes next.

Stay out of flood water

Floodwater can hide hazards and carry contamination. Do not wade in it, and do not run electronics in a wet area.

Cut power only if it is safe

If the panel is dry and reachable, shut off power to wet areas. If not, call the electric utility or 911 before you call us.

Move belongings up

Take medications, documents and valuables to an upper floor or a high shelf.
