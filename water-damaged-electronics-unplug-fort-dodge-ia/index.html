Water Damaged Electronics: When to Unplug in Fort Dodge

Power is the danger. Cut it at the source, stay out of standing water and let a professional inspect anything that got wet.

By the Fort Dodge Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026

Water and electricity are a bad mix, and the risk is not only to your devices. Wet outlets, cords and appliances can shock the person who touches them. Before you worry about a laptop or a television, make sure you can reach the item without standing in water.

This guide explains what to do first and what to leave alone. It is general safety information and not a substitute for a licensed electrician.

Safety before salvage

If water is standing around outlets, power strips or appliances, do not walk into it. Turn off power at the breaker panel only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground. If the panel is wet or the floor is flooded, stay out and call your utility or an electrician.

Never touch a plugged-in device while standing in water. Unplug only what you can reach safely and dry. If in doubt, leave it and get help.

Small electronics

  1. Unplug the device, remove batteries and take off any cases.
  2. Do not turn it on to test it.
  3. Wipe the outside dry and set it upright in a dry, airy place.
  4. Leave it for several days, since moisture hides in circuit boards.
  5. Have a technician inspect anything valuable before powering it up.

Rice is a popular trick, but it does not do much. Open air and time do better. Saltwater and flood water corrode contacts quickly, so contaminated devices are often lost.

Large appliances

Refrigerators, washers, dryers and ovens contain wiring and motors. If one sat in water, do not plug it in. A technician should check the controls and wiring before it runs again. Our page on appliance leak damage covers what we do when a machine fails.

Water heaters and furnaces in a flooded basement need professional inspection before restart. Gas appliances in particular should not be relit by you.

Outlets, cords and the panel

  • Replace cords and power strips that were submerged.
  • Have outlets and switches in wet walls inspected before use.
  • Do not use an extension cord through water.
  • Treat any tripped breaker after a flood as a warning, not an inconvenience.
  • Call an electrician if the panel was wet at all.

We dry the building, and the electrician certifies the wiring. Both steps matter. For wall cavities, see wall and drywall repair.

Phones, laptops and tablets

Modern devices are sealed, but the seals fail over time. If a phone or laptop got wet, power it off, remove the case and take out the SIM tray and any removable battery. Do not charge it. Charging a wet device is the fastest way to turn a small problem into a dead board.

Place the device in a spot with moving air. A shelf near a fan works. Wait at least a day, and longer for a laptop. Then try to power it on. If it behaves oddly, shut it down and take it to a repair shop.

Back up your data as soon as the device works again, and consider whether it is safe to keep using it.

Why corrosion shows up days later

Water leaves minerals and, in floods, dirt and chemicals. These form a film on circuit boards. The film can corrode contacts slowly, so a device that works on Monday may fail by Friday.

That is why technicians clean boards with solvents and inspect them under magnification. A device that seemed fine can still need service.

If the water was from sewage or a river, treat electronics as a loss unless they are very valuable. Contamination makes safe cleaning difficult, and your insurer may cover replacement.

Data and backups

For many people the data matters more than the device. If a laptop or hard drive got wet, do not keep trying to turn it on. Each attempt can do more damage. A data recovery specialist can often retrieve files from a drive that is not powered.

Cloud backups are the best defense. If you did not have one before the loss, set one up after you get a working device.

Keep the damaged device in a dry bag until you decide what to do. Do not open it unless you know how, and avoid cleaning it with water or household cleaners.

The National Electrical Code and the American Red Cross both publish public guidance on flooded electrical systems, and an electrician applies the code locally.

Fort Dodge note: who to call for power

Utilities differ by town, so check your latest bill for the name of your electric provider and call them if service lines or the meter were underwater. Do not remove a meter yourself.

Fort Dodge's Code Enforcement Division at 819 1st Ave S administers the adopted building, mechanical and plumbing codes, so ask that office whether repairs to electrical systems need permits. People in Eagle Grove and Badger should ask their own city hall.

Questions from Fort Dodge homeowners

Can I plug in a wet device to see if it works?

No. Powering a wet device can short it and shock you. Dry it completely and have it checked.

Is rice helpful for a wet phone?

Not much. Open, moving air and time work better, and a repair shop can clean corrosion.

When is it safe to turn the power back on after a flood?

Only after an electrician has inspected the system. Do not guess.

Who do I call in Fort Dodge if the panel was in water?

Call a licensed electrician and your electric utility. Stay away until they say it is safe.
