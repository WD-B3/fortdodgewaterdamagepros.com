Downtown Fort Dodge is a mixed commercial district with older masonry buildings. This page covers water cleanup for storefronts, offices and apartments above shops.

Downtown Fort Dodge is a National Register historic district. The National Park Service lists it as added on November 10, 2010, bounded by 1st Avenue North, Central Avenue and 1st Avenue South, from 3rd Street on the west to 12th Street on the east. It covers the central business district, mainly along Central Avenue.

The National Park Service record lists architecture and commerce as the areas of significance, with 1854 and 1964 as significant years. A 2023 Messenger News report on a downtown facade project described buildings from 1880 and a seven-story building at 801 Central Avenue, the Snell Building, built in 1915.

Mixed use means mixed water problems

A Messenger News report from August 2023 noted that a downtown building at 700-702 Central Avenue has a shop on the first floor and apartments on the second. Buildings like that put homes and businesses under the same roof, so a leak can affect both.

We do not have a source describing basements, drainage or flooding for the downtown district. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. We make no claim that downtown is prone to flooding. Check any address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

Storefronts, offices and apartments above shops

Water that starts on an upper floor tends to move down through ceilings, wall cavities and stairwells. A roof or plumbing leak above can reach a shop below, with stock, fixtures and flooring in the way. A first-floor sprinkler or pipe failure can spread across a large open floor.

SituationWhat usually matters first
Leak from an upper-floor apartmentStop the source, protect the shop below, check ceilings and walls
Roof or flashing leakContain the water, then trace where it traveled
Pipe or fixture failure in a shopShut off supply, protect stock, extract and dry the floor
Sewer backupKeep people away, wait for protective gear

Older masonry buildings and drying

The 2023 facade report described downtown buildings dating from 1880, 1896 and 1915. We do not know the construction details of any one building, and older masonry and wood framing can hold moisture in ways that are hard to judge by eye, so we measure rather than guess.

Tell us the age of the building, the number of floors and who owns each unit. Owners, tenants and property managers all have a stake, and we need to know who can approve work. See structural drying for how readings guide the job.

Safety steps for owners and tenants

  • Close the water supply to the affected unit if you can reach the valve safely.
  • Keep staff, customers and residents away from wet floors and from electrical hazards.
  • Do not operate breakers while standing in water.
  • Cover or move stock and records above the wet floor if it is safe.
  • Photograph the damage and the source before cleanup.

If the water is dirty or came from a sewer line, keep everyone away until the crew arrives with protective gear.

Documentation for insurance

Business and residential policies differ, and coverage depends on the cause of the loss. Call your insurer or agent early and keep receipts. We provide photos, readings and a written scope. We do not decide coverage, and we never promise that a claim will be approved.

Landlords and tenants should both notify their insurers, since the building and the contents are often insured separately. For larger losses, see flood damage cleanup.
