How to Prevent Water Damage While Away from Your Fort Dodge Home

A short checklist before you travel can stop a small leak or a frozen pipe from becoming a house full of water.

Most big water losses start small and run unattended. A supply hose lets go on a Tuesday, nobody is home until Sunday, and the house takes in hours of water instead of minutes. Time is the whole difference between a mop-up and a gut job.

If you are leaving town for a weekend, a winter trip or a long stay somewhere warm, a few minutes of prep lowers the odds a great deal. This checklist covers the places water most often comes from, and it works for any season.

Shut off the water at the main

The single best step is to close the main water valve before you leave. With the main closed, a burst pipe, a failed hose or a cracked fitting has nothing to push water through. Test the valve before the trip, because old valves can stick or leak when turned.

If you have a water heater, turn the heat setting down or switch it to vacation mode according to the manufacturer's instructions. A heater with no water supply can be damaged if it keeps heating, so follow the label.

Check the appliances that hold water

  • Washing machine. Turn off the supply valves and replace rubber hoses with braided steel.
  • Dishwasher. Check the supply line and the area under the unit for dampness.
  • Refrigerator. Inspect the ice maker line and the drip pan.
  • Water heater. Look for rust, puddles and corrosion on the fittings.
  • Water softener. Check the drain line and the brine tank area.

Hoses older than five or six years are worth replacing. Write the date on new ones with a marker so you know when to swap them. Our guide to appliance leak damage shows what a failed line can do.

Protect the basement

A basement fails quietly because nobody sees it. Test the sump pump by pouring a bucket of water into the pit and watching the float rise and the pump shut off. Check that the discharge line is clear and points away from the foundation.

A battery backup is cheap compared with a flooded basement, since storms often cut power exactly when the pump is needed. Keep valuables off the floor and use shelves with legs. For what happens after a flood, see basement water damage cleanup.

Winter trips need extra care

If you are away during a cold snap, the biggest risk is a frozen pipe that splits. Leave the heat on, set no lower than 55 degrees and open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls so warm air reaches the pipes. Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses before the first hard freeze.

If you will be gone a long time in winter, consider draining the plumbing system completely and shutting off the heater. Ask a plumber about the right steps for your house. When a pipe does freeze, frozen pipe damage explains what comes next.

Ask someone to check in

A neighbor, a relative or a house sitter who stops by every few days can catch a problem early. Give them the main valve location, a flashlight, the phone number for your plumber and ours. Tell them what to look for, which is a damp smell, a drip, a stain or a running sound.

Smart leak sensors under sinks and near the water heater can send an alert to your phone. They are inexpensive and they work best when someone can act on the alert. Do not rely on one device alone.

Roof, gutters and the outside of the house

Water also comes from above and outside. Clean gutters and downspouts before you leave so rain does not overflow near the foundation. Make sure downspout extensions carry water several feet away from the house. Check the roof for missing shingles or damaged flashing, especially after wind or hail.

Look at the grade around the foundation. Soil should slope away from the walls. Low spots near the house collect water and push it toward the basement. A bit of soil added before a storm season can make a difference.

Inspect window wells and make sure covers are secure and drains are clear.

If you are gone for a long stretch

For a seasonal home or a long absence, set a check schedule. Ask someone to walk through weekly, run a faucet briefly and check ceilings, basements and the water heater. Give them written instructions and a number to call.

Consider shutting off the main, draining the lines and turning off the water heater if the home will sit empty. A plumber can advise on the best steps for your system, especially in freezing weather.

Make a short list of emergency contacts and tape it near the main shutoff. Include your insurer's claim line so whoever finds a problem can start the process quickly.

Fort Dodge note: winter and spring thaw

Iowa winters bring hard freezes, and a thaw can turn a frozen pipe into a flood the moment it warms up. If you leave Fort Dodge in winter, ask a neighbor to check the house after any big temperature swing. The same applies in nearby towns like Badger and Humboldt.

For river and creek questions, check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurance agent about flood and water backup coverage before the season starts.

Questions from Fort Dodge homeowners

Should I shut off the main water when I leave for a weekend?

It is a good habit for longer trips and costs nothing. For short trips, at least shut the washing machine valves.

What temperature should I leave the heat at in winter?

Many sources suggest no lower than about 55 degrees. Follow your plumber's advice for your home.

Do leak sensors work?

They can give early warning, but only if someone sees the alert and can act on it.

What if I find water when I come home to Fort Dodge?

Shut off the main if you can reach it safely, stay away from electricity and call us. We can start extraction and drying right away.
