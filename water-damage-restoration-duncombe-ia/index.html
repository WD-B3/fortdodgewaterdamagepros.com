When water is spreading in Duncombe

In a town this size, you may be the only person who knows the water is there, so the first minutes are yours. Do the following while the call goes through.

Shut the main valve

Find where the water line enters the house and close it. In older small-town homes, the shutoff may be in a basement corner or a crawl space.

Make power safe

Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and reachable. Otherwise call the electric utility or 911.

Lift and move

Raise furniture off wet carpet and move small valuables to a counter or a second floor.

Call the line

Describe the room, the source and how much water. The person on the phone will tell you what to do next.

The problems we see most often

Small-town water losses tend to be ordinary ones. A supply line splits behind a wall, a water heater tank fails, a washing machine hose lets go or a sump pump quits during a rainstorm. Each is covered on its own page, such as burst pipe cleanup, water heater leak cleanup and basement water damage cleanup.

What differs in a small town is access. Distances are longer, so an early call matters more. If you also have a wet floor that needs drying after the water is out, structural drying follows extraction.

Duncombe is a small city in Webster County. For permit and code questions about repairs after a water loss, ask Duncombe city hall first and the county if the city directs you there. Do not assume Fort Dodge rules apply, because each jurisdiction has its own office.
