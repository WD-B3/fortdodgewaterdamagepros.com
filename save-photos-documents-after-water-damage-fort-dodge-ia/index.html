How to Save Photos and Documents After Water Damage in Fort Dodge

Fast action and a freezer can save paper that looks hopeless. Here is the order of steps that gives you the best chance.

Few losses sting like wet family photos. Birth certificates, wedding albums, tax records and old letters all matter, and they are all made of paper. Paper can survive water better than most people think, if you move quickly and avoid a few mistakes.

This guide covers the basics for photos, documents and books. For truly irreplaceable or valuable items, a professional document recovery service is worth a call.

First, safety and speed

Make sure the area is safe before you reach in. Do not touch wet items near live electrical outlets. If the water was contaminated, wear gloves, and consider that sewage or flood water makes paper a health risk.

Speed matters most. Photos and documents start to stick together and stain within a day or two, so move them out of the wet area quickly.

Photos: how to handle them

  1. Handle by the edges only and never rub the surface.
  2. Rinse gently in clean water if mud or dirt is on them.
  3. Lay them face up on a clean surface, such as paper towels or a screen.
  4. Do not stack wet photos. Separate prints that are stuck together underwater if they come apart easily.
  5. Dry in a room with moving air, away from direct sun.

If you cannot deal with them quickly, put them in a bag and freeze them. That pauses the damage until you can thaw and dry them gently.

Documents and books

Do not pull stuck pages apart. Interleave wax paper between wet pages if you can and freeze the item. Freezing buys time. A frost-free freezer can also dry paper slowly over weeks, which is a mixed blessing.

For single documents, blot and air dry flat. For books, stand them on end with the pages fanned out. Copies of important documents are cheap, so scan or photograph them once dry.

Drying paper without a freezer

If you can deal with items right away, air drying works. Spread paper towels or clean unprinted newsprint on a table and lay documents flat in a single layer. Change the towels when they get damp. Keep a fan moving air across the room but not directly on the paper.

Do not use an iron or heater to speed things up. Flatten dried pages under a clean, heavy book. Ink may bleed on some papers, so work gently and do not rub.

Expect some cockling and staining. That is normal and usually acceptable for records you only need to read.

Making digital backups going forward

Once your papers are dry, scan them. A phone scanning app is enough for most records. Store copies in two places, such as a cloud account and an external drive kept somewhere else.

For photos, scan prints at a good resolution and label the files with names and dates. This also protects you from fire and theft.

Keep a small fireproof and waterproof box for originals that matter most, like birth certificates and deeds, and store it above the floor in a closet on an upper level.

When to call a document recovery specialist

Some items justify professional help. If you have a large volume of wet records, rare books, valuable prints or business documents you cannot replace, ask a document recovery company about freeze drying and other methods.

Ask what they can do, how long it takes and what it costs. Your insurer may cover recovery of valuable papers, so ask about that before you pay.

Until you reach them, keep the items cold or frozen and avoid handling them more than needed.

Think about records you need for claims

Insurance and tax records matter most. Photograph or scan what you can, and ask your insurer whether you can request copies of policies and statements online. Many banks and agencies keep copies, so you may not have lost the only version.

If your electronics got wet, our guide on appliance leak damage covers appliances, and the unplugging question is worth asking an electrician. For documents in a basement flood, see basement cleanup.

For valuable records, the Library of Congress and the National Archives publish public guidance on salvaging wet paper, and it is worth reading before you start.

Fort Dodge note: replacing vital records

If an important record is lost, ask the issuing office for a replacement. Birth and marriage records in Iowa come from the state and county offices that issued them. Call ahead to learn what ID and fees apply, since requirements change.

Property records, whether for a home in Fort Dodge or a house in Humboldt or Gowrie, are kept by the recorder in the county where the property sits, so you can usually ask for copies without the original.

Questions from Fort Dodge homeowners

Does freezing really help wet photos?

Yes. Freezing stops mold growth and sticking so you can deal with them later, one at a time.

Should I use a hair dryer on wet photos?

No. Heat can curl and crack the surface. Use gentle air movement instead.

Can wet hard drives be recovered?

Sometimes, but do not power them on. Contact a data recovery specialist.

Where can I get a replacement for a lost document in Fort Dodge?

Contact the office that issued it. Many records can be requested by mail or online.
