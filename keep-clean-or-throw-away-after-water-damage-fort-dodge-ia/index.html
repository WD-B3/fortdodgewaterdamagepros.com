Keep, Clean or Throw Away After Water Damage in Fort Dodge

What you can save depends on the item, the type of water and how long it sat wet. This guide sorts common belongings.

By the Fort Dodge Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026

After a flood or a pipe break, the hardest part is often the stuff. Furniture, clothes, books, toys and bedding all look the same once they are wet. Deciding what to keep is emotional, and it is also a health and insurance question.

The general rule is that clean-water items that were wet briefly can often be saved, while porous items touched by contaminated water usually cannot. Here is how that plays out, item by item.

Start with the water type

Everything depends on where the water came from. Clean water from a supply line is the best case. Gray water from an appliance is riskier. Black water from sewage or a river is the worst case, and most porous items touched by it should be thrown away. Our explainer on sewage backup cleanup covers that side.

Time matters as much as the source. Items wet for more than a day or two have a much lower chance of recovery, even with clean water.

Items that can often be saved

  • Solid wood furniture that dries slowly and evenly.
  • Glass, ceramic, metal and hard plastic after cleaning and disinfecting.
  • Clothing and linens from clean water, washed promptly.
  • Framed photos and documents if frozen or dried quickly.
  • Appliances that were not powered when wet, after a technician checks them.

Dry items slowly and away from direct heat. Fast heat can warp wood and crack finishes.

Items that are usually thrown away

  • Carpet pad and most padding.
  • Upholstered furniture soaked by gray or black water.
  • Mattresses and pillows exposed to contaminated water.
  • Particleboard furniture that swelled.
  • Food, medicine and cosmetics that touched floodwater.
  • Stuffed toys and porous items from sewage or river water.

When in doubt, photograph the item first, list it for your insurer and then dispose of it safely.

How to clean items worth saving

For items that can be saved, move them to a dry, airy place. Remove drawers and doors from furniture so air reaches the inside. Wipe surfaces with a mild detergent and clean water. Disinfect hard, nonporous surfaces if the water was gray or black, following the product label.

Wash clothing and linens promptly in the hottest water the fabric allows. If they cannot be washed right away, keep them damp in a bag rather than letting them dry stained, then wash as soon as possible.

Dry wood slowly. Fast heat or direct sun can crack veneers and warp panels. Patience gives wood the best chance.

Special items that need a specialist

Antiques, heirloom rugs, fine art, musical instruments and valuable books are worth a specialist's time. Conservators and restoration firms with experience in contents can often save items that look hopeless.

Ask about the process, the expected outcome and the cost before you commit. Compare that to the replacement cost and the sentimental value.

Photograph the item in its current state and keep it in a cool, dry place while you decide. Do not apply cleaners or heat before a specialist has seen it.

Working through a room without getting overwhelmed

Large losses feel paralyzing. Break the work into small piles. Make one pile for items to save now, one for items to decide on later and one for items to discard. Work one shelf or one closet at a time.

Take breaks and ask a friend to help carry and sort. Having a second set of eyes makes decisions easier, and a second set of hands lifts the heavy things.

Keep a running list as you go. It speeds up your insurance inventory and helps you remember what you moved where.

Document before you discard

Take photos of anything you plan to throw out and list it with an age and value. Keep small samples or labels where useful. Your insurer may ask to see large items before disposal, so ask first.

Pack salvageable items in a clean, dry space. Our guide on carpet restoration explains how rugs and carpets get sorted, and hardwood repair covers flooring.

The water categories used to sort belongings come from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

Fort Dodge note: disposal questions

Large loads of wet debris need somewhere to go. Ask the City of Fort Dodge or your waste hauler what rules apply to bulky items after a flood, and whether special collection is offered. Rules vary by town, so call before you fill the curb.

The same questions apply in Duncombe and Eagle Grove, where your own city hall sets the pickup rules.

Questions from Fort Dodge homeowners

Can I save a mattress after a clean water leak?

It is hard to dry a mattress through and through. Most are replaced, especially if wet for long.

Can I keep furniture from a flood?

Solid wood from clean water often can be saved. Furniture from contaminated water usually cannot.

What about food in the freezer?

If power failed or water reached it, follow food safety guidance and discard anything doubtful.

How do I dispose of wet items in Fort Dodge?

Ask the city or your waste hauler about bulky waste rules before you set items out.
