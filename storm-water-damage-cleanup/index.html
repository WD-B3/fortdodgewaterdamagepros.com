When storm water is an emergency

Storm damage can be unsafe in several ways. Downed power lines, unstable ceilings and wet wiring are all possible. Do not enter areas with active hazards, and do not climb onto a damaged roof.

Stay away from downed lines

Call the utility or 911 about any downed or sparking lines.

Make the area safe

Keep people away from sagging ceilings and wet electrical equipment.

Temporary protection

A tarp over a damaged roof section or a board over a broken window can slow further water. Do not climb on a wet roof.

Photograph everything

Take photos of the damage before and after temporary measures for your insurance file.

Storm calls at any hour are routine. The line is open 24/7, though road conditions can affect timing.

How storm water gets in

  • Roof damage. Missing shingles, damaged flashing or a limb through the deck.
  • Windows and doors. Broken glass or failed seals in driven rain.
  • Siding and walls. Wind-driven rain forced behind siding.
  • Gutters and drainage. Overflow directs water toward the foundation.

Once inside, water behaves like any other. It soaks insulation, drywall and flooring, so the interior work is extraction and drying, as described in water extraction and structural drying.

Temporary protection versus repair

Board-up and tarping stop more water from entering. They are temporary measures, and a roofer or a contractor makes the permanent repair. Restoration handles the interior, and the exterior repair is its own job.

Insurance and storm claims

Wind and hail damage is often covered by a homeowners policy, and the interior water damage that follows is typically part of the same claim. Flood is a separate matter, and most standard policies exclude it. The Iowa Insurance Division publishes guidance for homeowners after storms. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

Document the damage before temporary repairs, keep receipts for tarps and board-up and avoid signing a contract on the doorstep with anyone who knocks after a storm. Take your time to compare.

What affects the cost of storm water cleanup

Storm losses are priced by what the water reached:

  • Entry point: A roof opening can soak insulation and ceilings
  • Amount of rain: Longer exposure means more wet material
  • Materials: Insulation, drywall and flooring each have their own scope
  • Temporary protection: Tarps and board-up are separate
  • Drying days: Equipment time

Many storm losses are covered when sudden, but check your policy or ask your insurer. Want your real number? Call the line.

Questions about storm water damage in Fort Dodge

Do you handle storm water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Fort Dodge?

Yes. The 24/7 line is open during and after storms. Same-day response is offered when available, but road and weather conditions can affect timing.

Should I tarp my roof first?

If it is safe, a temporary cover can help. Do not climb on a wet or damaged roof. A roofer can tarp it, and we can dry the interior.

Does insurance cover storm damage?

Often for wind and hail, and for the resulting interior water damage. Flood is usually excluded. Check your policy.

Should I trust door-to-door contractors after a storm?

Be careful. Compare more than one estimate, ask about licensing and insurance, and avoid paying large amounts up front.
