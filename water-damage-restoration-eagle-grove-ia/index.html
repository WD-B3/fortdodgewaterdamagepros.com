Eagle Grove, IA is a city of 3,601 people in Wright County, platted in 1881 near the Boone River. It is named for the eagles that once nested in an oak grove along the river, and the river is still part of how the area handles water.

Our 24/7 emergency line reaches Eagle Grove from Fort Dodge, about 19 miles to the southwest, and same-day response is offered when available. Because Eagle Grove is in Wright County, it has its own city offices and county permit rules, separate from Fort Dodge and Webster County.

A sewer backup page of its own

The City of Eagle Grove runs a water utility and posts its own guidance on sewer backups. That is useful to know. The page is the place to start if you think a blockage is in the city main. If water or sewage comes up through a floor drain or toilet, read sewage backup cleanup and stay out of the area.

If the blockage might be in the city main, contact the city utility. If it is in your own lateral, a plumber handles it. We handle the contaminated water and the affected materials.

When water is spreading in Eagle Grove

Eagle Grove homeowners can follow the same safe steps whether the water is clean or not.

Stop using water

If sewage is backing up, stop running sinks, showers and washing machines.

Close the main valve for clean leaks

If a pipe has failed, closing the main valve stops the flow.

Stay clear of electrical hazards

If water reached outlets or a panel, call the utility or 911 first.

What to have ready before you call from Eagle Grove

Give the dispatcher your street address, the room that is wet and the source if you know it. Say whether the water is still flowing and whether anyone is hurt or unable to leave. The answers decide whether the first move is shutoff, power safety or extraction.

Mention warning signs such as a bulging ceiling, a water line near the electrical panel or sewage odors. Take a few photos before moving furniture, since they help the claim and the plan.

Tenants in Eagle Grove should notify the property owner right away and ask who is authorized to order the cleanup. Keep your own list of damaged belongings for a renters policy.

Why drying continues after the floor looks dry

Water moves into baseboards, wall bottoms, subfloors and insulation within hours. The surface can feel dry while those materials stay wet. Equipment keeps pulling moisture out until readings reach the dry standard we set at the start.

We record those readings every visit. If you need the log for an insurance file, ask for a copy before the equipment comes out.
