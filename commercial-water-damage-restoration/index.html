The restoration steps are the same as in a home, which start with extraction and move to drying, but the planning is different. This page explains how a commercial loss is handled.

When a commercial water loss is an emergency

In a business, every hour of water also costs revenue, and some losses affect people other than the owner. Call as soon as you can safely do so, and focus first on safety.

Protect people

Move employees and customers away from wet areas, and keep an eye on slippery floors and electrical hazards.

Shut off the source

Close the valve for the affected line or the main. In a multi-unit building, tell the property manager at once.

Power and fire systems

Do not touch wet electrical equipment. Call the utility or 911 about electrical risk, and do not disable fire safety systems.

Protect inventory and records

Lift stock off the floor and move records to dry areas, if it is safe to do so.

Night, weekend and holiday calls are normal. Same-day response is offered when available.

What makes commercial jobs different

  • Scale. Larger floor areas need more equipment and more planning.
  • Occupancy. Tenants and customers may need access while work continues.
  • Inventory and contents. Stock, records and equipment may need moving or drying.
  • Business interruption. Speed matters because the business may be closed.
  • Multiple stakeholders. Owners, tenants, managers and insurers all need information.

Documentation is more important too. Photos, moisture logs and a written scope help owners, tenants and insurers see the same facts.

How we plan the work

We start with a walk-through to identify the source, the affected area and what is at risk. The crew sets priorities, usually extraction first, then drying the most critical areas, then the rest. In occupied buildings, work can be staged to keep some areas open.

In a multi-unit building, water often travels between units. A leak in an upper apartment can drip into a unit below, so the plan includes checking neighbors. The written scope shows each area, the equipment and the monitoring plan so everyone is working from the same information.

What affects the cost of commercial water damage work

Commercial jobs are priced by scope:

  • Affected area: Large floor areas need more equipment
  • Water category: Contaminated water increases cleaning and removal
  • Materials: Commercial flooring, ceilings and walls vary
  • Working hours: Staging to keep the business open can add time
  • Drying days: Equipment time drives cost

A commercial property policy may cover sudden water damage, but exclusions for flood, sewer backup and long-term leaks are common. Check your policy or ask your insurer. Want your real number? Call the line.

Safety steps while you wait

Commercial drying follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. Until the crew arrives, these steps keep people safe.

  • Clear staff and customers from wet areas and block off slippery floors.
  • Shut off the water supply and call your building engineer or property manager.
  • Photograph inventory and equipment before it is moved.
